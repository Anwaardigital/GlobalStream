How to Check Your Electricity Bill Online in Pakistan (2026 Guide)

By Anwaar Digital · Last updated: 24 November 2026 · Bills & everyday life

Every month, millions of households in Pakistan wait for the same piece of paper: the electricity bill. If it arrives late, gets lost, or is damaged by rain, many people still travel to the distribution office just to get a duplicate copy. That trip is no longer necessary. Every electricity distribution company in Pakistan now lets you check your bill online — free, in under a minute — using a single number printed on every bill.

This guide shows you exactly how to check your electricity bill online in Pakistan: where to find your reference number, the step-by-step process for your company (LESCO, IESCO, FESCO, MEPCO, GEPCO, PESCO, HESCO, SEPCO, QESCO or K-Electric), how to read the charges on your bill, how to pay safely online, and what to do when something goes wrong. No app download is needed, and no fee is charged for checking.

What you need: your 14-digit reference number

Everything starts with one number: the 14-digit reference number. It is printed on every electricity bill, usually near the top, and it stays the same every month — even when the amount changes. Your company uses it to find your connection, your meter and your billing history. Without it, you cannot check your bill online.

If you have any old bill — even one from last year — the reference number on it still works. Keep one old bill in a safe place at home, or save a photo of it on your phone, so you always have the number ready. You will also need to know which company supplies your electricity (see the table below) and a phone or computer with internet.

One warning: your reference number is not a password, but it is still personal. Do not post photos of your bill on social media or share the number in public groups. Anyone with the number can see your bill — and scammers use bill photos to build fake payment messages. Keep it private, the same way you would keep any household document.

How to check your bill online — step by step

The process is almost the same for every distribution company. It takes less than a minute:

  • Step 1 — Find your company. Check the table below, or look at the name printed on your paper bill.
  • Step 2 — Open your company's official website. Search for the company name plus "duplicate bill" or "online bill". Make sure you are on the real company website — lookalike sites with similar names exist, and they may show you wrong information or try to take your payment.
  • Step 3 — Find the bill-check page. It is usually called "Duplicate Bill", "Online Bill", "View Bill" or "Bill Inquiry". It is normally on the homepage or in the customer services menu.
  • Step 4 — Enter your 14-digit reference number. Type it carefully, with no spaces or dashes. Some sites ask you to pick your company or region first — choose the one that matches your bill.
  • Step 5 — View your bill. Your current bill appears on screen: the amount due, the due date, the units consumed and the billing month. Check that the name and address match yours before you trust the amount.
  • Step 6 — Download or print a copy if you need one. Most sites offer a PDF duplicate bill you can save or print. It works the same as the paper bill for payment at a bank or shop.

That is all there is to it. If you are travelling, check your bill before you leave so a missed due date does not turn into a late-payment charge — the same habit as checking live flights before a trip. And if the amount surprises you, do not pay in a hurry: read the bill first, using the next sections of this guide.

How to read your bill

A bill looks confusing at first, but it has only a few parts that matter:

Units consumed

This is the difference between your current meter reading and the previous one. It is the number that decides almost everything else on the bill. Compare it with last month's units first — if the units are similar but the amount jumped, the cause is usually a tariff or tax change, not a mistake.

Tariff slab

Electricity is priced in slabs: the price per unit rises as your usage rises. Using more units can push you into a higher slab, where every extra unit costs more. That is why a bill can jump sharply in summer, when fans and air conditioners run all day. Check the weather forecast during a heatwave and you will usually see the reason for a high bill coming.

Taxes and surcharges

Several charges sit on top of the units you used: GST, the fuel price adjustment (FPA), the FC surcharge, a TV licence fee and sometimes meter rent or a quarterly tariff adjustment. The FPA moves with fuel prices and the dollar rate — you can follow the live dollar rate on GlobalStream to understand why it changes. Tariff changes are usually announced in the news first; the Pakistan news page is a good place to catch them.

Due date and late payment surcharge

Every bill has a due date. Pay after it, and a late payment surcharge is added — usually a percentage of the bill. Pay before it, and you pay only the bill itself. If you pay late one month, the surcharge appears on the next bill as well, so a single late payment can cost you twice.

Arrears

Arrears are any unpaid amount carried over from previous months. If you see arrears you do not recognise, check your old bills and payment receipts before paying — a payment made at a shop or bank can take a day or two to appear on the system.

How to pay your bill online

Once you have checked the amount, you can pay without leaving home:

  • Your bank's mobile app or website. Almost every Pakistani bank lets you pay utility bills: choose "Electricity", pick your company, enter your reference number, confirm the name and amount, and pay. Save the transaction ID or receipt.
  • Mobile wallets. JazzCash and Easypaisa both have bill-payment options that work the same way — enter the reference number, confirm, and pay.
  • The company's own portal. Some companies accept card payments directly on their website.
  • ATM or bank branch. If you prefer cash, the printed duplicate bill works at banks and payment shops exactly like the original.

Whatever method you use, pay before the due date, and keep the receipt until the payment shows on your next bill. Never pay through a payment link sent to you by SMS or WhatsApp — real companies do not collect payment that way. If a message claims your electricity will be cut in a few hours unless you pay at a link, it is a scam. The free guides on CyberSec Hub explain how to spot these fake messages before you click.

Common problems — and how to fix them

  • "Bill not showing yet." Bills are generated on a fixed cycle each month. If your bill is not online yet, check again after a day or two — it is not lost, it is simply not generated.
  • "Reference number not found." Retype the number carefully from a recent bill, with no spaces or dashes, and check you selected the right company. One wrong digit is the most common cause.
  • The name on the bill is wrong, or it shows a previous tenant. The online check still works — the reference number is what matters. To correct the name, visit the company office with your CNIC and proof of ownership or tenancy.
  • The website will not load. Try again later, try a different browser, or check during off-peak hours. Company websites slow down badly on the days just before the due date, when everyone checks at once.
  • The bill is much higher than usual. Compare the units with last month, not just the amount. A long gap between meter readings can push you into a higher slab. If the reading itself looks wrong, read your own meter, note the number with the date, and contact the company with that evidence.
  • The bill is estimated, not actual. If the meter reader could not reach your meter, the company may issue an estimated bill. The next actual reading corrects it — but if estimates continue for months, report it, because a wrong estimate can hide a real problem.

Five ways to keep your bill down

  • Set the air conditioner to 26°C. Every degree lower adds noticeably to the bill. A fan running with the AC lets you stay comfortable at a higher setting.
  • Switch to LED bulbs. They use far less electricity than old bulbs and last much longer — the saving shows up within a month or two.
  • Unplug chargers and devices on standby. A charger left in the socket, a TV on standby and a UPS running all day all draw power around the clock.
  • Service your appliances. A dirty AC filter or an old fridge working too hard can add hundreds of units over a summer. Clean filters and door seals cost almost nothing.
  • Think long term about solar. Solar panels are a big upfront cost, but for households with high daytime usage they can cut the bill sharply. Get quotes from more than one installer, check warranties, and do the sums before deciding — money saved on electricity is money that can go into savings, whether that is a bank account or an asset like gold.

Scams to avoid

  • Fake disconnection messages. "Your electricity will be cut in 2 hours — pay now at this link." Real companies send a proper bill first and do not threaten disconnection by SMS link. Delete the message.
  • Fake bill-payment websites. Sites that copy the look of a company website and take your payment — and your card details. Only use the official website, your bank's app, or a wallet you already trust.
  • Calls asking for your OTP or bank details. No electricity company will ever call and ask for your OTP, PIN or card number. Anyone who does is a scammer.
  • "Discount" offers. Messages promising a 50% discount on your bill if you pay through a special link are always fake. There is no such scheme.

If you are unsure about any message, check your real bill online first — the official amount and due date are the truth, and anything that disagrees with them is suspect. Frequently asked questions

Can I check my bill without the reference number?

No. The reference number is the only way to look a bill up online. If you have lost every old bill, visit your company's nearest office with your CNIC and your address, and they can give you the number.

Is checking my bill online free?

Yes. Checking — and downloading a duplicate bill — is completely free on every company's website. You only pay the bill itself, never a fee for looking at it.

Can I check someone else's bill?

Only if you have their reference number, and only with their permission — for example, checking a parent's bill for them. Do not share your own reference number publicly.

What is the fuel price adjustment (FPA)?

It is a monthly charge that reflects changes in fuel costs for power generation. When fuel gets more expensive, the FPA rises; when fuel gets cheaper, it falls. It is shown as a separate line on your bill, and it is one of the main reasons the same number of units can cost a different amount from one month to the next.

What happens if I miss the due date?

A late payment surcharge is added to your bill, and it also appears on the next bill if it stays unpaid. If a bill stays unpaid for a long time, the company may send a disconnection notice. Paying even a few days late is better than not paying at all — and checking your bill online means you always know the date.

Does the duplicate bill work for payment?

Yes. A duplicate bill printed or downloaded from the official website is accepted at banks and payment shops exactly like the original paper bill.

The bottom line

Checking your electricity bill online in Pakistan takes less than a minute: find your 14-digit reference number on any old bill, open your company's official website, enter the number, and your bill is on screen — amount, due date and units, with a free duplicate copy to download. Read the bill before you pay, pay before the due date through your bank or a trusted wallet, and never trust a payment link from a random SMS.
