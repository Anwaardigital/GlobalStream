Travelling in Pakistan: How to Plan Your Trip with Live Information (2026 Guide)

By Anwaar Digital · Last updated: 16 November 2026 · Travel & live data

Pakistan has mountains taller than most countries, a desert, a long coastline, 5,000 years of history, and some of the warmest hospitality a traveller can find. Yet many people — including Pakistanis planning a trip inside their own country — find the planning harder than the travelling. Which month is best? How do you get from Lahore to Hunza? How much cash should you carry, and where do you check the weather before driving into the mountains?

This guide answers those questions in plain English. It does not print flight times, prices or weather numbers, because those change every day. Instead, it shows you how to plan each part of the trip — and where to check the live information on GlobalStream before you leave and while you travel. Use it for a family holiday, a first visit from abroad, or a weekend trip to the northern areas.

When to visit Pakistan

Pakistan has four clear seasons, and the best month depends on where you are going. The northern mountains and the southern plains are almost opposite: when Karachi is hot and humid, Hunza is pleasant; when Hunza is snowed in, Lahore is enjoying its best weather.

  • October to March — the best months for most of Pakistan. The plains and cities are cool and comfortable. This is the best time for Lahore, Islamabad, Karachi, the desert around Bahawalpur, and the historical sites of Sindh and Punjab.
  • April to June — hot in the plains, perfect in the north. The northern areas open up as the snow melts. This is the season for Hunza, Skardu, Naran, Swat and the Karakoram Highway. Cities in the south become very hot, so plan indoor activities for midday.
  • July to September — monsoon season. Heavy rain can close mountain roads and cause landslides in the north, and cities can flood after a strong downpour. If you travel in these months, check the live weather forecast every morning before you set out, and keep a day of spare time in your plan.

The rule is simple: check the weather for your exact destination, not for "Pakistan" in general. A forecast for Karachi tells you nothing about the weather in Murree. GlobalStream shows the forecast city by city, so check each stop on your route separately.

Getting to Pakistan

Most international visitors arrive by air. Pakistan's main international airports are Islamabad, Lahore and Karachi, with smaller international flights into Peshawar, Multan, Faisalabad, Sialkot and Quetta. Direct flights connect Pakistan with the Gulf, the United Kingdom, Europe, Turkiye, Malaysia, Thailand and China, and connecting flights reach almost everywhere else.

Before you book, check three things:

  • Your flight's live status. Delays happen, especially in winter fog in Lahore and Islamabad. The GlobalStream live flights page shows departures and arrivals in real time, so you can see whether your flight is on time before you leave for the airport.
  • Visa rules. Many nationalities can apply for a visa online before travelling. Check the current rules on the official government visa website well before your trip — rules change, and this guide does not try to keep up with them. Apply early and keep a printed copy of your approval.
  • The currency rate. If you are bringing foreign currency, check the live exchange rate before you change money. The rate on the day you land is the rate that matters, not the rate from a guide written months ago.

If family or friends are meeting you at the airport, share your live flight status with them. The live flights page updates automatically, so they can see a delay as soon as it happens instead of waiting at the airport for hours.

Getting around inside Pakistan

Pakistan is a large country — Karachi to Islamabad is about 1,400 kilometres by road — so the way you travel matters more than in a small country. Four options cover almost every trip:

1. Domestic flights

Flights connect Karachi, Lahore, Islamabad, Peshawar, Quetta, Multan, Gilgit and Skardu. A flight that takes one hour replaces a 15-hour road journey, and in the north, flying into Gilgit or Skardu avoids the long and sometimes difficult mountain road. Mountain flights depend on the weather and can be cancelled at short notice, so check the live flight status and the weather on the morning of the flight, and never plan a tight connection on the same day.

2. Trains

Pakistan Railways runs between the main cities — Karachi to Lahore, Lahore to Rawalpindi, and on to Peshawar. Trains are slow but comfortable and cheap, and the journey itself is part of the experience, especially the route through the Punjab countryside. Book a sleeper or air-conditioned class for overnight journeys, and arrive early: stations are busy and platforms are not always clearly announced.

3. Buses and coaches

Private coach companies run comfortable air-conditioned buses between cities, often more punctual than the trains. For shorter trips — Lahore to Islamabad, Islamabad to Murree — coaches and vans are the usual choice. On mountain roads, travel in daylight only, and in monsoon season check the weather before departure, because landslides can close the road for hours.

4. Cars, ride-hailing and the motorway

The motorway network links Lahore, Islamabad, Peshawar and Multan with smooth, fast roads. Ride-hailing apps work well in the big cities and are safer than hailing a taxi on the street, because the fare is fixed before you ride. If you hire a car with a driver for a northern trip, choose a driver who knows the mountain roads, agree the total price in advance, and confirm whether fuel and tolls are included. Keep the weather forecast and Pakistan news open while you travel, so a road closure or a storm warning reaches you before you reach the road.

Where to go: five regions worth your time

Pakistan is too big to see in one trip. Pick one region and enjoy it properly instead of rushing through five. These are the five most popular choices:

Islamabad and the Margalla Hills

The capital is green, calm and easy to walk around, with the Faisal Mosque, the Margalla Hills trails and the markets of the old city of Rawalpindi nearby. It is the best base for a first visit: safe, organised, and close to the mountains. Two or three days are enough to see the city properly.

Lahore — history and food

Lahore is the cultural heart of Pakistan: the Badshahi Mosque, Lahore Fort, the Walled City, and food streets that stay busy late into the night. The best time is October to March, when the weather is cool enough to walk the old city. Lahore's winter fog can delay flights, so check the live flight status if you are flying in or out.

Karachi — the sea and the city

Karachi is Pakistan's biggest city and its port: beaches at Clifton and Hawksbay, the Quaid-e-Azam mausoleum, and markets that sell everything. The sea breeze makes it cooler than the inland cities in summer, but the humidity is high. Karachi is also the best place to see Pakistan's business and street life up close.

Hunza, Gilgit and Skardu — the high mountains

The Karakoram mountains hold some of the tallest peaks on Earth. Hunza's valleys, the Attabad Lake, Skardu's cold desert and the road to the Khunjerab Pass are the most photographed places in Pakistan. The season is roughly May to October. Roads are mountain roads: check the weather every day, carry warm clothes even in summer, and allow spare days for weather delays.

Swat and Kalam — the green valleys

Swat is closer and easier to reach than Hunza, with green valleys, rivers and cool summer weather. It is a good choice for a family trip of three or four days from Islamabad. In monsoon season, check the forecast and the news before driving into the valley, because heavy rain can flood the river roads.

Money: rupees, cards and cash

Pakistan runs on cash. The rupee (PKR) is the currency, and small shops, taxis, roadside restaurants and mountain guesthouses usually take cash only. Cards work in big hotels, malls and restaurants in the cities, but do not rely on them outside the cities.

  • Carry enough cash. ATMs exist in every city but are rare in the mountains. Withdraw cash in a city before heading north, and keep it in more than one place.
  • Check the exchange rate live. If you are changing dollars, pounds or euros, the rate moves every day. Check the live currency page on the day you change money, and compare the bank rate with the money changer's rate before you accept one.
  • Agree prices before you buy a service. Taxi fares, boat rides and guided walks are often negotiated. Agree the price first, in rupees, and confirm what it includes. A polite question before the ride is worth more than an argument after it.
  • Keep some small notes. Rickshaws, tips and small purchases are easier with 100 and 500 rupee notes than with a 5,000 note nobody can change.
  • Watch the gold rate if you shop for jewellery. Pakistan's bazaars sell beautiful gold jewellery, but prices follow the daily gold rate. Check the live gold rate and read the gold rate guide before you buy, so you know what a fair price looks like.

Staying safe and connected

Pakistan is welcoming, and most trips pass without any trouble. A few simple habits cover almost all of the risk:

  • Buy a local SIM card. Mobile data is cheap and coverage reaches most towns and valleys, though signal fades in remote mountains. A local SIM gives you maps, ride-hailing and live weather and flight updates wherever you go. Register it properly at the shop with your passport or ID.
  • Keep your documents safe. Carry a copy of your passport or ID and keep the original in your hotel safe. Take a photo of your documents and keep it on your phone as a backup.
  • Stay aware of the news. Before travelling to a new area, check the Pakistan news page and the world news page for anything that affects your route. GlobalStream reports the news from original sources, including reporting on Palestine, so you can follow events as they are reported rather than through rumours.
  • Beware of online scams. Fake hotel listings, fake tour operators and "too cheap" travel deals are common on social media. Never pay in advance to a stranger, and check a website before you pay — the free guides on CyberSec Hub explain how to spot a fake site. If a deal looks too good to be true, it almost always is.
  • Respect local customs. Dress modestly, especially outside the big cities and near mosques and shrines. Ask before photographing people. During prayer times, shops may close briefly — the live prayer times page tells you when, so you can plan your visits around them. Read the prayer times guide if you want to understand how the timings are calculated.
  • Drink safe water and eat carefully. Drink bottled water, eat at busy restaurants where the food is fresh, and be careful with street food in the first days of a trip until your stomach adjusts.

What to pack

  • Layers, not bulk. Mountain mornings are cold even in summer. A warm jacket, a light rain jacket and comfortable walking shoes cover most trips.
  • Sun protection. The sun is strong at altitude. Sunscreen, sunglasses and a hat are essential in the north.
  • Cash and a power bank. Cash for the mountains, and a charged power bank because long drives and remote valleys have few charging points.
  • Basic medicines. Carry your regular medicines plus pain relief, rehydration salts and something for an upset stomach. Pharmacies are easy to find in cities but not in remote valleys.
  • A small day bag. Water, snacks, your documents, your phone charger and a little cash — everything you need for a day out, without carrying your whole suitcase.

A simple 7-day plan for a first trip

If you have one week and you have never visited Pakistan, this plan covers the highlights without rushing:

  • Days 1–2: Islamabad. Arrive, rest, see the Faisal Mosque and the Margalla Hills, and adjust to the time zone.
  • Days 3–4: Lahore. Fly or drive to Lahore. Spend one day in the old city and the fort, and one day eating your way through the food streets.
  • Days 5–7: Hunza or Swat. Fly to Gilgit or drive to Swat, and spend three days in the valleys. Keep the last day flexible — mountain weather can change your plans, and a spare day turns a delay into a rest day instead of a missed flight.

Check the weather and the flight status each morning of the trip, and check the exchange rate if you need to change more money along the way. If you only have a long weekend, skip the north and do Islamabad plus Lahore — two cities, no mountain roads, and a relaxed pace.

Frequently asked questions

Is Pakistan safe for tourists?

Millions of Pakistanis travel inside the country every year, and foreign visitors are increasing. The main tourist areas — Islamabad, Lahore, Hunza, Skardu and Swat — are visited by families every season. As with any country, stay aware of the news, follow local advice, avoid travelling alone on remote roads at night, and keep your documents and cash secure.

What is the best month to visit the northern areas?

May to October is the usual season, with June to September the warmest and greenest. Passes can close in winter, and monsoon rain in July and August can delay travel, so check the live weather forecast for your exact destination before you go.

Do I need cash, or do cards work?

Both — but cash is essential. Cards work in city hotels, malls and larger restaurants. Small shops, taxis, mountain guesthouses and bazaars take cash only. Withdraw rupees in a city before travelling north.

What should I check each morning of the trip?

Four things: the weather for your destination, the flight status if you are flying, the prayer times for your city, and the news for anything affecting your route. Five minutes in the morning saves hours later.
