Gold Rate in Pakistan: How to Read It and What Affects It (2026 Guide)

By Anwaar Digital · Last updated: 10 November 2026 · Gold & money

In Pakistan, the gold rate is one of the most checked numbers of the day. Families check it before a wedding, savers check it before buying, and shopkeepers check it before opening their shops. Yet many people do not know what the number actually means — what a tola is, why the rate changes every day, and why two shops sometimes quote two different prices.

This guide explains the gold rate in Pakistan in plain English: how it is set, what moves it, how to read it correctly, and how to check the live rate for free on GlobalStream's gold rate page. No jargon, no guesswork — just what you need to know before you buy or sell.

What is the gold rate in Pakistan?

The gold rate is the price of a fixed weight of gold, quoted in Pakistani rupees. In Pakistan, gold is almost always quoted per tola. One tola equals 11.6638 grams — roughly the weight of 10 one-rupee coins and a little more. Jewellers also quote per 10 tola and per gram, so the same rate can look very different depending on the unit. If a shop quotes Rs 346,000 per tola, that is about Rs 29,670 per gram (346,000 ÷ 11.6638). Free online calculators, such as the tools on Anwaar Free Tools, can do this conversion for you in seconds — but it helps to know the maths yourself so no one can confuse you with units.

Gold is also sold in different purities. 24 karat is pure gold (99.9%). 22 karat is the most common for jewellery in Pakistan (about 91.6% pure, mixed with a little copper or silver for strength). 21 karat and 18 karat are less pure and therefore cheaper. When you see a rate, always ask which karat it is for — the headline rate is usually for 24 karat, and 22 karat jewellery will be priced lower. On the GlobalStream gold rate page you can see 24k, 22k, 21k and 18k rates side by side, so the difference is clear.

How is the daily rate set?

The Pakistan gold rate is not decided by one person or one shop. It is built from three ingredients:

  • The international gold price. Gold is traded worldwide in US dollars per troy ounce (31.1 grams). When the world price rises, the Pakistan price usually rises too.
  • The dollar-to-rupee exchange rate. Because gold is priced in dollars internationally, the rupee price moves with the USD to PKR exchange rate. If the rupee weakens against the dollar, gold becomes more expensive in rupees even if the world price has not moved. You can check the live dollar rate on GlobalStream's currency page.
  • The local sarafa market. The All Pakistan Sarafa Gems and Jewellers Association publishes a daily rate that jewellers across the country follow. This is the number most shops quote, and it usually tracks the international price plus the exchange rate, with small local adjustments for demand and supply.

That is why you will sometimes see two numbers for the same day. On the GlobalStream gold rate page, the international spot price and the Pakistan sarafa estimate are shown separately and clearly labelled. The spot price is the live world market price converted to rupees; the sarafa estimate is the local market rate. They are usually close, but they are not the same thing — and knowing which one you are looking at stops a lot of confusion.

What affects the gold price?

Gold does not move randomly. Six forces push it up or down:

1. The dollar rate

This is the biggest short-term factor in Pakistan. When the rupee falls against the dollar, gold gets more expensive in rupees. When the rupee strengthens, gold usually gets cheaper. That is why gold buyers watch the live currency rates just as closely as the gold rate itself.

2. The international gold price

World gold prices react to interest rates, inflation and central-bank buying. When big economies cut interest rates, gold often rises because holding gold becomes more attractive than holding cash in a bank. When rates rise, gold often cools down.

3. Demand — weddings and festivals

In Pakistan, demand jumps during the wedding season and before Eid. More buyers in the market can push the local rate slightly above the international level. The same happens in India, the world's second-biggest gold market — when Indian demand rises, it affects prices across South Asia.

4. Inflation and uncertainty

People buy gold when they worry about inflation or about their currency losing value. Gold is seen as a safe store of value: it does not depend on any one government or bank. When world news turns worrying — wars, trade disputes, financial crises — investors move money into gold and the price climbs.

5. Central banks

Central banks, especially in China, India and the Middle East, have been buying large amounts of gold in recent years. That steady buying supports the price even when private demand is quiet.

6. The crypto alternative

Some younger investors now split their savings between gold and cryptocurrency such as Bitcoin. Crypto is far more volatile than gold — it can move 10% in a day — while gold usually moves slowly. Gold remains the calmer, safer choice for most families; crypto is a higher-risk bet. How to read the rate (spot vs sarafa, and the extra charges)

When you check the rate, remember three things:

  • Spot price vs sarafa estimate. The spot price is the live world price. The sarafa estimate is the local market rate. Use the sarafa estimate for buying in Pakistan; use the spot price to understand which way the market is moving.
  • Making charges. The quoted rate is for the gold itself. Jewellery costs extra: the shop adds making charges (mazdoori), usually a few thousand rupees per tola, plus GST. Always ask for the making charges separately — two shops with the same gold rate can have very different final prices.
  • Buy-back rate. When you sell gold back, the shop pays slightly less than the selling rate. Ask the buy-back rate before you buy, not after. A fair shop will tell you both numbers upfront.

Buying tips — and scams to avoid

  • Compare two or three shops. Rates should be close. A shop quoting far below the market rate is a warning sign, not a bargain.
  • Check the weight in front of you. Ask the shop to weigh the item on its scale while you watch, and check the karat stamp on the piece.
  • Get a proper receipt. It should show the weight, karat, rate per tola, making charges and total. Keep it — you will need it when you sell.
  • Beware of "too cheap" online offers. Fake gold-selling websites and social-media sellers are common. They take advance payment and disappear, or send plated metal instead of real gold. If you buy or sell anything online, learn how to spot a fake website first — the free guides on CyberSec Hub explain how to check a site before you pay.
  • Buy from a known jeweller. A shop with a long local reputation has more to lose from cheating you than a stranger does.
  • Keep gold safe. A bank locker is safer than a drawer at home. Insure it if the amount is large.

Frequently asked questions

What is 1 tola of gold in grams?

One tola equals 11.6638 grams. To convert any tola price to a per-gram price, divide by 11.6638.

Why is the gold rate different in different cities?

Small differences come from local demand, transport and shop costs. The sarafa association rate is the same across Pakistan, but individual shops may add slightly different making charges.

Is gold a good investment in 2026?

Gold has protected savers from inflation for centuries, but its price also falls at times. Most financial advisers suggest gold as a part of your savings — not all of it. Never borrow money to buy gold, and never put all your savings into a single asset.

What is the Zakat nisab for gold?

The nisab is 7.5 tola (about 87.48 grams). If you own that much gold or more for a full lunar year, Zakat of 2.5% is due. The GlobalStream gold rate page shows the current nisab value in rupees.

The bottom line

The gold rate in Pakistan is set by the world price, the dollar rate and the local sarafa market — and it moves every day. The smart way to follow it is to check the live rate with its last-updated time, know which karat you are looking at, and separate the gold price from making charges. Check the live gold rate, watch the dollar rate, and keep an eye on world news — together, those three tell you almost everything about where gold is heading.
