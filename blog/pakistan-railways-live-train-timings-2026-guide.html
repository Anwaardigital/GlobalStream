Pakistan Railways Live Train Timings: How to Check Train Schedules and Track Trains (2026 Guide)

By Anwaar Digital · Last updated: 22 November 2026 · Travel & live information

Every day, thousands of people across Pakistan travel by train — students going home for the weekend, families visiting relatives, workers commuting between cities, and traders moving goods. And every day, the same question is asked at railway stations, in WhatsApp groups and on phone calls: what time is my train, and is it on time?

Pakistan Railways runs trains between Karachi, Lahore, Rawalpindi, Peshawar, Quetta, Multan, Faisalabad, Hyderabad, Sukkur and dozens of smaller stations. But a printed timetable is only half the story. Trains can run late, schedules can change, and a train that left on time yesterday may be delayed today. That is why knowing how to check live train timings matters more than memorising a timetable.

This guide explains, in plain English, how to check Pakistan Railways train schedules, how to track a train's live running status, how to read a timetable properly, and how to book tickets — without printing a single train time that will be out of date tomorrow. Train times and fares change, so this guide shows you where to check them live, every time you travel.

Why checking live matters more than a printed timetable

A railway timetable tells you the scheduled time — the time the train is supposed to arrive and leave. It does not tell you what is happening right now. In Pakistan, trains can be delayed by fog in winter, by crossings, by maintenance work, or by congestion on a single-track section. A delay of one train can affect the trains behind it for hours.

Checking live before you leave home saves you from three common problems:

  • Waiting for hours at the station. If your train is running two hours late, you can leave home later instead of sitting on a cold platform.
  • Missing your train. If a train is running early or the platform has changed, a live check tells you before it is too late.
  • Planning connections. If you are changing trains, or catching a flight or a bus after your train arrives, you need the real arrival time — not the scheduled one. For onward air travel, check live flight tracking on GlobalStream the same way.

The rule is simple: check the schedule when you plan, and check the live status on the day you travel.

How to check Pakistan Railways train timings

There are four reliable ways to check train timings. Use the official sources first — they are the only ones that carry the railway's own data.

1. The official Pakistan Railways website

The official website is pakrail.gov.pk. On the site you can search for trains between two stations, see the scheduled departure and arrival times, check which days of the week a train runs, and see the classes available on that train. This is the same information the booking counters use, so it is the most trustworthy source for planning a journey.

When you search, you need three things: your departure station, your destination station, and your date of travel. The site shows every train that runs on that route, with times and classes. Write down the train's name and number — you will need the number to track it later.

2. The Pakistan Railways helpline

If you do not have internet access, call the railway helpline. The helpline can confirm timings, running days and seat availability for a particular train. Keep your train number and date ready before you call, so the person on the phone can answer quickly. The helpline is especially useful when you are already on the road and cannot browse the website.

3. Train-tracking apps

Several mobile apps show live train running status for Pakistan Railways — where a train is right now, how late it is running, and its expected arrival at your station. The most widely used is the Pak Rail Live style of app, available on Android. These apps work by combining the official schedule with user reports and GPS data, so they are very useful — but treat them as a guide, not as gospel. Always cross-check a critical journey (a connection, a wedding, a flight) with the official website or helpline as well.

4. The station enquiry counter

Every major station has an enquiry counter and display boards. The boards show the expected arrival and departure times, including delays. If you are already at the station, the display board and the announcements are the most up-to-date information you will get. Do not rely only on the printed timetable pasted on the wall — it shows scheduled times, not live ones.

How to read a railway timetable

A timetable looks confusing the first time you see it, but it only has a few pieces of information. Once you know them, you can read any train's schedule:

  • Train number and name. Every train has a number and a name — for example, the Karakoram Express, the Green Line Express, the Tezgam, the Khyber Mail, the Awam Express, the Pakistan Express, the Karachi Express and the Jaffar Express. The number is the important part for tracking: two trains can have similar names, but the number is unique.
  • Departure and arrival stations. Check the full route, not just the two ends. Your train may start in Karachi and end in Peshawar, but you may be boarding at Hyderabad or Multan — the time that matters is the time at your station.
  • Days of running. Not every train runs every day. Some trains run daily, others run only on certain days of the week. Always check the running days for your date — a train that runs on Monday may not run on Tuesday.
  • Arrival and departure at each station. At most stations the train stops for only a few minutes. The timetable shows both the arrival time and the departure time. If the halt is short, be on the platform before the train arrives.
  • Classes. Pakistan Railways trains usually offer Economy class, AC Business class, AC Sleeper and AC Parlour (on some trains). The class affects the fare and the comfort, not the timing — all classes on the same train arrive together.

One more thing to check: whether the train is up or down. Trains going towards Karachi are usually called "down" trains, and trains going away from Karachi are "up" trains. This matters when you ask at the enquiry counter — the same train name can have two different numbers, one for each direction.

Major routes and famous trains

Pakistan Railways' main line runs from Karachi up through Hyderabad, Sukkur, Multan, Lahore and Rawalpindi to Peshawar — the country's busiest railway corridor. Most long-distance trains run on this line, and it connects the largest cities in the country.

Some of the best-known trains on this network include the Green Line Express (Karachi to Islamabad, known for its limited stops and comfortable coaches), the Karakoram Express (Karachi to Lahore), the Tezgam and Khyber Mail (Karachi to Peshawar), the Awam Express and Pakistan Express (Karachi to Rawalpindi), the Karachi Express (Karachi to Lahore), the Jaffar Express (Quetta to Peshawar), the Shalimar Express (Karachi to Lahore) and the Millat Express. Branch lines connect Quetta, Faisalabad, Sargodha, Sialkot, Narowal and many smaller towns.

You do not need to memorise these names. What matters is the route: find the trains that run between your two stations, compare their timings and travel time, and pick the one that suits you. A train with fewer stops is usually faster but may not stop at your station — so check the stop list, not just the start and end points. For longer trips, it also helps to check the weather forecast for your destination on GlobalStream, especially in winter when fog can delay trains across Punjab.

How to track a train live

Tracking a train live means finding out where it is right now and how late it is running. Here is how to do it step by step:

  • Note the train number from your ticket or from the official website. The number is more reliable than the name.
  • Open a live train-tracking app or website and enter the train number and today's date.
  • Read the status carefully. The app will show the train's last reported station, how many minutes late (or on time) it is running, and the expected arrival at your station.
  • Check again closer to departure. A train that is one hour late at Multan may make up time — or lose more time — by Lahore. Check once in the morning and once an hour before you leave home.
  • Confirm at the station. Display boards and announcements are the final authority. If the app and the board disagree, trust the board.

If you are receiving someone at the station, tracking the train live is even more useful: you can time your arrival at the station to the train's real arrival, instead of waiting for hours. And if your journey continues after the train — a connecting bus, a taxi to the airport — the live status lets you warn the other side in time. For flights, the same habit applies: check live flight status before leaving for the airport.

How to book a train ticket

Booking a ticket is separate from checking timings, but the two go together — you usually check the timings first, then book. Pakistan Railways offers online booking through its official e-ticketing service on pakrail.gov.pk, where you can select your train, class and seat, and pay online. You can also book at the reservation counter at any major station, or through authorised booking agents.

A few practical points:

  • Book early for popular trains and dates. Seats on trains like the Green Line Express fill up quickly, especially before Eid, on weekends and during the wedding season.
  • Check the advance-booking window on the official site. The number of days ahead you can book changes from time to time, so check the current rule when you book rather than relying on what it was last year.
  • Keep your ticket safe. Whether it is a printed ticket or an e-ticket on your phone, keep it until the end of the journey. Ticket checkers on the train will ask for it.
  • Check the fare on the official site. Fares change when fuel prices change, so never rely on an old fare. For the same reason, check the live petrol price if you are comparing the cost of the train with driving or taking a bus.
  • Book from the official source only. Be careful of touts and unofficial agents who charge extra or sell fake tickets. If a website or a person asks for advance payment outside the official booking system, stop and verify first. Why trains run late — and what to do about it

Delays are the most common complaint about Pakistan Railways, and they have real causes. Understanding them helps you plan better:

  • Winter fog. From December to February, thick fog across Punjab slows trains down for safety. Morning trains are affected most. If you travel in winter, build extra time into your plans and check the fog and weather forecast the night before.
  • Single-track sections. Much of the network is single track, so trains must wait at stations for a train coming the other way to pass. One late train can delay several others.
  • Maintenance and engineering work. Track repair and bridge work sometimes slow trains or divert them. This is usually announced in advance on the official website.
  • Crossings and level crossings. Trains slow down at busy crossings, especially near cities.
  • Busy stations. At big junctions like Lahore, Rawalpindi and Multan, trains wait for platforms to clear.

What can you do? Travel with a buffer: if you have a wedding, a flight or an exam the same day, take an earlier train, not the last one that could work. Carry water, food and a phone charger, because a delay can turn a six-hour journey into ten. And if your train is delayed by hours, ask at the enquiry counter about your options — sometimes another train on the same route leaves sooner. Knowing the prayer times for your journey also helps you plan stops and breaks on a long trip.

At the station: practical tips for a smooth journey

  • Arrive early. Reach the station at least 30 to 45 minutes before departure. Platforms are announced close to departure time, and finding your coach takes time — coaches are marked by class and number.
  • Check the coach and seat number. Your ticket shows the coach (for example, Economy coach 5) and the seat or berth number. Look for the coach number painted on the side of the coach, not just the train name.
  • Keep luggage light and labelled. Put your name and phone number on your bags. Keep valuables — phone, wallet, documents — on your person, not in luggage stored overhead.
  • Buy food before boarding. Food is available at stations and from vendors on the train, but it is safer and cheaper to carry your own water and snacks, especially on long journeys.
  • Charge your phone. Charging points are limited on trains. A full battery and a power bank matter — your phone is your ticket, your tracker and your contact with family.
  • Listen to announcements. Platform changes happen. The station announcements and display boards are the official source; a fellow passenger's guess is not.
  • Keep your ticket until you leave the station. You may need to show it at the exit gate.

If you are travelling with children or elderly family members, book a lower berth or a seat near the door, and ask the station staff for help with luggage. Railway staff at major stations can help — ask politely and clearly.

Staying safe: scams and fake bookings to avoid

  • Never buy a ticket from a stranger at the station entrance. Touts sell fake or used tickets at inflated prices. Use the counter or the official website.
  • Do not pay in advance to an unknown "agent" on WhatsApp or Facebook. Real booking happens through pakrail.gov.pk or at the counter. Advance payment to a stranger is how most train-ticket scams work.
  • Check the website address. Fake railway booking sites copy the official site's design. The official domain is pakrail.gov.pk — check the spelling carefully before entering payment details. CyberSec Hub has free guides on checking whether any website is genuine before you pay.
  • Keep your CNIC and ticket together. You may be asked for identification on the train.
  • Be careful with your belongings at night. On overnight trains, keep your bag where you can see it or use the chain and lock provided under the seat.

Frequently asked questions

Where can I check live Pakistan Railways train timings?

On the official website pakrail.gov.pk, through the railway helpline, on live train-tracking apps, or on the display boards at the station. Check the schedule when you plan and the live status on the day you travel.

How do I track a train's live running status?

Note the train number from your ticket, enter it in a train-tracking app or website with today's date, and read the last reported station and the expected arrival at your station. Confirm with the station display board before boarding.

Why is my train late?

The most common reasons are winter fog, single-track waiting, maintenance work and congestion at busy junctions. Check the live status rather than guessing — the delay shown on the day is the only number that matters.

Can I book Pakistan Railways tickets online?

Yes — through the official e-ticketing service on pakrail.gov.pk. You can also book at reservation counters at major stations. Book early for popular trains and holiday dates.

Which is the fastest train from Karachi to Lahore?

It depends on the day and the current schedule, because timings and stop patterns change. The Green Line Express and the Karakoram Express are among the fastest on this route, but always compare the trains running on your date on the official website — that is the only way to get the right answer for your journey.

Do trains run on time in winter?

Winter fog often delays morning trains across Punjab. Build in extra time, check the weather forecast the night before, and check the live status again on the morning of travel.

Is it safe to travel by train in Pakistan?

Millions of people travel by train every year. Book through official channels, keep your ticket and ID with you, look after your luggage, and follow station announcements. For families, the train is often the most comfortable way to travel between cities.

The bottom line

Pakistan Railways is still one of the best ways to travel between Pakistan's big cities — cheap, comfortable, and now easier to follow than ever. The trick is not to trust a printed timetable alone: check the schedule on pakrail.gov.pk when you plan, track the train live on the day you travel, and confirm at the station before you board.
