How to Check How Many SIMs Are Registered on Your CNIC (2026 Guide)

By Anwaar Digital · Last updated: 27 November 2026 · Mobile & identity safety

Your CNIC number is linked to every mobile SIM you own in Pakistan — and, sometimes, to SIMs you have never seen. A SIM registered in your name can be used to make calls, receive OTP codes, open mobile wallets and even commit fraud, all while the bill of responsibility points back at you. That is why checking how many SIMs are registered on your CNIC is one of the simplest and most important safety checks you can do.

This guide explains the check in plain English: the free SMS method to 668, the PTA website method, how to read the result, what to do if you find a SIM you do not recognise, and how to block extra SIMs at a franchise. No technical knowledge is needed, and the whole check takes less than five minutes. No SIM numbers are printed here — every CNIC has its own result, so the guide shows you where to check yours live.

Why should you check your SIMs?

Most people in Pakistan have owned several SIMs over the years — a Jazz number from college, a Telenor number for work, a Zong data SIM, a Ufone number that came free with a package. Some of those SIMs are still active in your name even though you stopped using them years ago. Others may have been issued with your CNIC details without your knowledge, sometimes by a dishonest shopkeeper who kept a copy of your ID card.

An unknown SIM in your name is a real problem, not a paperwork issue. It can be used for scam calls and messages, for receiving bank OTP codes, for mobile wallet fraud, or for criminal activity. If the police or a bank ever trace a number, they trace it to the CNIC it is registered against — which is yours. Checking regularly is the only way to catch this early.

There is also a legal limit. The Pakistan Telecommunication Authority (PTA) allows a maximum of five SIMs per CNIC for an individual. If unknown SIMs are using up your limit, you may be refused a new SIM when you genuinely need one — for example, when you buy a new phone or a data SIM for work. A quick check tells you exactly how many of your five slots are used and by which mobile operator.

Finally, the check protects your family. Parents often discover that a SIM was issued against their CNIC for a child's phone, and elderly relatives sometimes find SIMs they never asked for. Checking once every few months — and checking again after you lose a phone or change your number — is a habit worth keeping.

Before you start: what you need

You need only two things:

  • Your CNIC number — the 13-digit number on your Computerised National Identity Card, without dashes. You do not need the physical card for the SMS check, but you will need the original card if you have to visit a franchise later.
  • A mobile phone with any Pakistani SIM — for the SMS method, you can send the message from any mobile number. It does not have to be the number you are checking, because the check is done against your CNIC, not against the phone you send from.

Keep your CNIC number private while you do this. Do not type it into random websites, do not share it in WhatsApp groups, and never give it to someone who calls you claiming to be from PTA. The only two official places for this check are the PTA SMS service and the PTA website, both described below.

Method 1: Check by SMS to 668 (the easiest way)

The SMS method is the one most people in Pakistan use. It works on any phone, needs no internet, and takes about two minutes:

  • Step 1 — Open your SMS app and start a new message.
  • Step 2 — Type your 13-digit CNIC number in the message body, without dashes or spaces. For example, if your CNIC is 35202-1234567-1, type 3520212345671.
  • Step 3 — Send the SMS to 668. This is PTA's official short code for the SIM Information System.
  • Step 4 — Wait for the reply. You will usually get a reply within a minute or two. A small SMS charge applies, as with any text message.
  • Step 5 — Read the reply carefully. The reply lists, for each mobile operator, how many SIMs are registered on your CNIC — for example, Jazz: 2, Telenor: 1, Zong: 0, Ufone: 1. It does not show the full mobile numbers, only the count per operator.

If you get no reply, check that you typed all 13 digits correctly and that your phone has signal. Try again once. If it still does not work, use the website method below, or check with the mobile operator's helpline. Never pay anyone to do this check for you — it is a free PTA service apart from the ordinary SMS charge, and any shop or agent charging a fee for it is overcharging you.

Method 2: Check on the PTA website

If you prefer a screen to an SMS, PTA offers the same check on its website:

  • Step 1 — Go to the PTA website at pta.gov.pk. Type the address yourself in your browser — do not follow a link from a random message or comment.
  • Step 2 — Find the SIM Information System. Look for the SIM information or "Check SIMs on CNIC" service on the PTA homepage or in its consumer services section.
  • Step 3 — Enter your CNIC number (13 digits, without dashes) and submit the form. You may be asked to enter a code shown on the screen to prove you are not a robot.
  • Step 4 — Read your result. The page shows the same information as the SMS reply: how many SIMs each operator has registered against your CNIC.

The website method is useful when you want to keep a record — you can take a screenshot of the result and save it. But the SMS method is faster, so most people start with 668 and use the website only when they want a second confirmation.

How to read your result

Your result is a simple count per operator. Here is how to understand it:

  • Compare the total with the SIMs you actually use. Add up the numbers from all four operators. If the total is more than the SIMs you know about, one or more unknown SIMs are registered in your name.
  • Check each operator separately. If you only ever used Jazz and Telenor, but the result shows a Zong SIM as well, that Zong SIM needs investigating — even if the total looks small.
  • Remember old SIMs count too. A SIM you bought in 2019 and threw away may still be active in your name. Old SIMs are the most common surprise in this check.
  • Zero is a normal result for an operator you never used. Seeing 0 next to an operator is good news, not an error.
  • Five is the maximum. If your total is already five, you cannot get a new SIM on your CNIC until an old or unknown one is blocked. That is another reason to clear out SIMs you no longer use.

Do the check for every adult in your family. Many people find unknown SIMs only when a relative checks for the first time — and it is much easier to fix the problem now than after the SIM has been misused.

Found a SIM you do not recognise? Do this now

Finding an unknown SIM is worrying, but it is fixable. Work through these steps in order:

  • Step 1 — Do not panic, and do not ignore it. An unknown SIM does not mean you are in trouble, but leaving it active is a risk. Act the same day if you can.
  • Step 2 — Note the operator and the count. The SMS or website result tells you which operator holds the unknown SIM. That tells you exactly whose franchise to visit.
  • Step 3 — Call the operator's helpline. Call the helpline of the operator shown in your result — Jazz, Telenor, Zong or Ufone — and tell them a SIM is registered on your CNIC that you do not recognise. They can tell you when it was issued and which franchise issued it. Never trust a helpline number from a forwarded message; use the number printed on the operator's official website.
  • Step 4 — Visit the nearest franchise or service centre. Take your original CNIC with you. Tell the staff you want to block a SIM registered on your CNIC that is not yours. They will verify you with a fingerprint (biometric verification) and block the SIM.
  • Step 5 — Get written confirmation. Ask for a receipt or an SMS confirmation that the SIM has been blocked. Keep it — it is your proof that you reported the SIM and had it closed.
  • Step 6 — Check again after a day or two. Send the SMS to 668 again. The unknown SIM should now be gone from your count. If it is still there, go back to the franchise with your blocking receipt.
  • Step 7 — Complain to PTA if the operator does not help. If a franchise refuses to block the SIM, or the SIM reappears, file a complaint with PTA through its toll-free helpline or its online complaint system. Keep your CNIC number, the operator's name and your blocking receipt ready when you complain.

If you suspect the unknown SIM has already been used for fraud — for example, you received a bank message about a transaction you did not make — contact your bank immediately as well, and change your mobile banking PIN and passwords. How to block an extra or old SIM you no longer use

You do not need a fraud reason to close a SIM. If you have old SIMs eating up your five-SIM limit, close them the same way:

  • Visit a franchise or service centre of that operator with your original CNIC.
  • Ask for the SIM to be blocked or closed, and complete the fingerprint verification.
  • Take the confirmation receipt and check on 668 after a day or two that the count has dropped.
  • If the SIM is in a phone you still have, remove it after it is blocked so nobody can reuse the number by mistake.

A blocked SIM cannot be used for calls, messages or OTP codes. The mobile number is usually returned to the operator's pool after a waiting period and may later be given to someone else — that is normal, and it is another reason to update your number with your bank, your employer and your family whenever you change it. If you change your number, remember to check currency rates for any mobile wallet balance in rupees before you close an old wallet-linked SIM, so no money is left behind.

Old SIMs, family SIMs and SIMs of deceased relatives

Three situations cause most of the confusion in this check:

SIMs bought for children

Children under 18 cannot get a SIM on their own CNIC, so parents often register a child's SIM on their own CNIC. That SIM will show in your count, and that is legal and expected — but remember it uses one of your five slots. When your child turns 18 and gets their own CNIC, move the number to their name at the franchise so your limit is freed up.

SIMs you stopped using years ago

A SIM you abandoned is not automatically cancelled. Operators usually block a SIM only after a long period of no use, and even then the record can stay against your CNIC. If your 668 result shows an operator you have not touched in years, that is almost certainly an old SIM — visit the franchise and close it.

SIMs of a deceased family member

If a parent or relative has passed away, their SIMs are still registered on their CNIC until someone closes them. Take the death certificate and your own CNIC to the operator's franchise and ask for the numbers to be blocked. This stops anyone from misusing the number for OTP codes or mobile wallets linked to it, and it also protects the family's bank accounts if the number was used for mobile banking.

Stay safe: CNIC copies, OTP codes and fake PTA calls

The SIM check itself is safe, but the information around it is valuable to criminals. Keep these rules:

  • Never give your CNIC number to a caller. PTA never calls citizens to ask for CNIC numbers, OTP codes or fingerprints. If someone calls claiming to be from PTA, Jazz, Telenor, Zong or Ufone and asks for your CNIC or an OTP, hang up. It is a scam.
  • Never share an OTP with anyone. An OTP is the key to your bank account, your mobile wallet and your SIM itself. No real helpline, bank or franchise will ever ask for it. If someone asks for it, they are trying to steal from you.
  • Be careful with photocopies of your CNIC. Every time you give a photocopy — to a SIM seller, a bank, an office — write the purpose and the date across it. A plain, undated copy can be reused to issue a SIM in your name.
  • Buy SIMs only from official franchises or authorised retailers. Roadside sellers who offer a SIM without biometric verification are breaking the law, and a SIM bought that way may be registered wrongly or resold. Always complete the fingerprint check and keep the receipt.
  • Do not click "check your SIM" links in SMS messages. Scammers send fake messages saying your SIM will be blocked unless you click a link. The real check is only by sending an SMS to 668 yourself, or by visiting pta.gov.pk directly. Any link in a message is suspicious.
  • Check after losing your phone. If your phone is stolen or lost, block the SIM immediately through your operator's helpline, then check your CNIC on 668 to make sure no replacement SIM was issued without your knowledge. Pakistan news and world news regularly report new mobile fraud tricks, so staying informed is part of staying safe.

If you travel abroad, remember that your Pakistani SIM keeps working on roaming and keeps receiving OTP codes — do not leave an unknown or extra SIM active while you are away, because you will not notice misuse until you return. Check the live flights page and the weather forecast when you plan a trip, and do your SIM check before you fly, not after.

Frequently asked questions

Is the 668 SMS check free?

The service itself is a PTA service, but your mobile operator charges the ordinary SMS rate for the message you send, so a small charge applies. There is no extra PTA fee, and nobody should charge you a separate fee for doing the check.

Can I check SIMs on someone else's CNIC?

No — and you should not try. The check is for your own CNIC only. Checking another person's CNIC without their permission is a misuse of their personal information. If you are helping an elderly parent, do it with them present and with their agreement.

How many SIMs can I have on one CNIC?

PTA allows a maximum of five SIMs per CNIC for an individual. The count covers all operators together, not five per operator.

What if the 668 reply shows a SIM I know I closed?

Wait a day or two and check again — blocking can take time to appear in the system. If the SIM is still showing after a few days, take your blocking receipt back to the franchise and ask them to confirm the closure.

Can I check my SIMs without a CNIC number?

No. The whole system works on the CNIC number, so you need the 13-digit number. If you have lost your CNIC, get a replacement from NADRA first — and while you wait, be extra careful about who holds a copy of your old card.

What should I do if a franchise refuses to block an unknown SIM?

Ask for the refusal in writing, note the staff member's name and the branch, and file a complaint with PTA. A franchise is required to block a SIM on the CNIC holder's request after biometric verification, so a refusal should be reported, not accepted.

The bottom line

Checking how many SIMs are registered on your CNIC takes two minutes: send your 13-digit CNIC number by SMS to 668, or check on the PTA website. Compare the result with the SIMs you actually use. If anything is unknown, visit the operator's franchise with your original CNIC and have it blocked — then check again to confirm it is gone. Repeat the check every few months, and always after losing a phone or changing your number.

Your SIM is your identity on the mobile network: it receives your bank OTPs, your wallet codes and your verification messages. Keeping your SIM list clean is as important as keeping your passwords safe. Before your next trip, check your SIMs along with the weather and the gold rate — the small checks are the ones that save you from big problems later.
