Live Parcel Tracking in Pakistan: How to Track Any Courier Delivery (2026 Guide)

By Anwaar Digital · Last updated: 26 November 2026 · Deliveries & live tracking

Every day, millions of parcels move across Pakistan — online shopping orders, documents, gifts from family abroad, and business deliveries. And almost every sender and receiver asks the same question: where is my parcel right now?

Live parcel tracking answers that question. You enter a tracking number, and you see where your parcel is, where it has been, and when it should arrive. This guide explains parcel tracking in Pakistan in plain English: how it works, where to find your tracking number, how to track with each major courier, what every status message means, and what to do when a parcel is stuck. No printed tracking numbers here — every parcel has its own number, so the guide shows you where to check yours live.

What is live parcel tracking?

When a courier accepts your parcel, it gives the parcel a unique tracking number (also called a consignment number or CN number). At every step of the journey — booking, sorting at a hub, loading onto a truck or plane, arriving at your city, and going out for delivery — a worker scans the parcel's barcode. Each scan is recorded with a time and a place, and the courier's website shows you those scans as a timeline.

That is all "live" tracking really is: a list of scans. It does not show your parcel on a map like a taxi, and the location is only as fresh as the last scan. If nothing has changed for a day, it usually means the parcel is sitting in a warehouse or on a truck between scans — not that it is lost. Understanding that one fact removes most of the worry people feel while waiting.

Tracking helps in three ways. First, you know whether your parcel is actually moving. Second, you know which day to stay home for delivery. Third, if something goes wrong, you have proof — a scan history you can show the courier when you complain. For online sellers, tracking is also proof that the order was delivered, which settles most payment disputes.

Where to find your tracking number

Your tracking number is the key to everything, so find it first. It is usually a mix of letters and numbers, often 10 to 14 characters long. Here is where to look:

  • On the booking receipt. When you send a parcel from a courier office, the receipt (and the sticker pasted on the parcel) shows the consignment number. Keep the receipt until the parcel is delivered.
  • In an SMS or email. Most couriers send the tracking number by SMS to the sender and sometimes to the receiver. Online shops do the same after dispatch.
  • In the shopping app. If you ordered from Daraz or another online store, open the order in the app and tap "Track order" — the tracking number and the live status are shown there. The same applies to international orders on AliExpress, which show the tracking number under each order.
  • From the sender. If someone sent you a parcel, ask them for the tracking number or a photo of the receipt. Without it, a courier can only search by phone number, and not every branch will do that.

Save the number in your phone's notes as soon as you get it. A quick trick: turn the tracking link into a QR code with a free tool such as the QR code generator on Anwaar Free Tools, so anyone in the family can scan it and check the same parcel.

How to track your parcel, step by step

Tracking any parcel takes less than a minute once you know the courier's name:

  • Step 1 — Identify the courier. The tracking number, the SMS, or the shop's order page tells you which company is carrying the parcel. If you are not sure, a web search for the first letters of the tracking number usually names the courier.
  • Step 2 — Go to the courier's official website. Every major courier has a "Track" box on its homepage. Type or paste the tracking number — without spaces — and press Track. Use the official site, not a random "tracking" site from a search ad, because fake tracking sites are used to steal phone numbers.
  • Step 3 — Read the timeline. The newest scan is at the top. Check the date and time of the last scan and the city it was in. That tells you where the parcel is and how recently it moved.
  • Step 4 — Check again later. Scans usually appear at night, when parcels are sorted at hubs. If you check in the morning and see no change, check again in the evening before worrying.

There are also universal tracking websites and apps that follow parcels from many couriers at once. They are handy when a parcel changes hands — for example, an international parcel that starts with one company abroad and finishes with Pakistan Post at home. But the courier's own website is always the most accurate source, because it gets the scans first.

Tracking with Pakistan's main couriers

Each courier in Pakistan has its own tracking page and its own way of showing statuses. Here is how the main ones work:

Pakistan Post

Pakistan Post handles regular mail, registered post, and its express services (UMS and EMS for international mail). You can track registered and express items on the Pakistan Post website with the number on your receipt. International parcels sent through EMS are tracked end to end — the same number works on the sender country's postal site and on Pakistan Post. Regular unregistered letters cannot be tracked, because they are never scanned.

TCS

TCS is Pakistan's largest private courier. Its tracking page shows a full timeline from booking to delivery, and it also offers SMS updates. TCS delivers documents and parcels across the country, including many smaller towns, and its overnight service between big cities is the one most businesses use.

Leopards Courier

Leopards is the other big private courier, with a similar tracking page and overnight delivery between major cities. Its tracking numbers are shown on the booking receipt, and its website and app both show the scan timeline.

M&P (Muller & Phipps)

M&P runs courier and logistics services across Pakistan and is often used by banks and companies for documents and cards. Its tracking works the same way: enter the consignment number on its website to see the scan history.

Daraz and online shopping orders

When you order from Daraz, the parcel is usually carried by Daraz's own logistics service (DEX) or by a partner courier. You do not need a separate tracking number: the Daraz app shows the live status — packed, shipped, arrived at your city, out for delivery — and the rider's name and phone number on the delivery day. If the order is "shipped" but the status does not move for two or three days, contact Daraz support from inside the app rather than guessing.

International parcels — AliExpress, DHL, FedEx and UPS

Parcels from abroad need more patience. A parcel from AliExpress is usually carried by Cainiao or another Chinese logistics company, then handed to Pakistan Post on arrival in Pakistan — so you track it on the AliExpress order page first, and on Pakistan Post once it lands. Express couriers such as DHL, FedEx and UPS show very detailed tracking, including customs clearance steps, and they usually call you if customs needs a document or a payment. International parcels can spend several days in customs, and that waiting time is normal — it is not the same as being lost. Customs duty is charged in rupees, so if you want to understand what the duty costs, check the live dollar-to-rupee exchange rate, since duty is often worked out from the dollar value of the parcel.

What each tracking status means

Tracking pages use short status phrases that confuse many people. Here is what the common ones mean:

  • Booked / Shipment received. The courier has accepted your parcel. The journey has started, but the parcel may still be at the booking office.
  • In transit / Departed. The parcel has left one city and is travelling to the next. Long gaps between "in transit" scans are normal on long routes such as Karachi to Gilgit.
  • Arrived at facility / Arrived at hub. The parcel reached a sorting centre. It will be sorted and sent on, usually the same night.
  • Arrived at destination city. The parcel is in your city. Delivery is usually the next working day.
  • Out for delivery. The rider has the parcel and is on the way to your address. This is the day to keep your phone charged and nearby.
  • Delivered. Someone signed for the parcel. Check who signed if you did not receive it yourself.
  • Delivery attempted / Consignee not available. The rider came but nobody answered. The courier usually tries again the next working day, or asks you to collect it from the branch.
  • Held at customs. An international parcel is waiting for customs clearance. The courier or customs may contact you for a document or duty payment.
  • Returned to sender. The parcel could not be delivered — wrong address, refused, or unclaimed — and is going back.

If the status has not changed for more than three working days on a domestic parcel, or more than seven days on an international one, it is time to contact the courier — see the next section.

Your parcel is stuck or delayed — what to do

Delays happen, especially in bad weather. Rain, fog and monsoon conditions slow trucks and close airports, and that shows up as a parcel sitting at one hub for an extra day. Flight delays have the same effect on express parcels that travel by air. Before assuming the worst, check the live weather and the world news and Pakistan news pages for floods, fog, strikes or road closures on your parcel's route.

If the parcel really is stuck, work through this checklist:

  • Confirm the address. One wrong digit in a phone number or a missing house number is the most common cause of a failed delivery. Check the address on the tracking page and ask the sender to confirm what they wrote.
  • Call the courier's helpline. Give the tracking number and ask for the parcel's last scan location. The helpline number is on the courier's official website — never trust a helpline number from a random comment or forwarded message.
  • Visit the nearest branch. For parcels marked "arrived at destination city", the branch can often hand the parcel over the same day if you show your ID card.
  • Ask the sender to complain too. The sender is the courier's customer, so a complaint from the sender's side often moves faster. Keep your receipt — it is your proof of booking.
  • For international parcels, check customs. If the status is "held at customs", contact the courier — they usually handle customs for you and will tell you what is needed.
  • Give it one more working day. Most "stuck" parcels move on the next sorting cycle. If nothing changes after that, file a written complaint and keep the complaint number.

Compensation rules differ by courier, and valuable items should always be booked with declared value and insurance. If you are sending gold jewellery or other valuables, read the gold rate guide first so you know the value you are declaring — and never send cash inside a parcel, because couriers do not cover it.

Delivery day: cash on delivery and checking your parcel

Most online orders in Pakistan are paid by cash on delivery (COD), so the delivery day matters. Keep these habits:

  • Keep your phone with you. The rider usually calls before arriving. A missed call can mean a missed delivery and a second trip the next day.
  • Have the exact amount ready. Riders often do not carry change. The delivery amount is shown in the shopping app, so check it before the rider arrives.
  • Check the parcel before paying. Look at the packaging: is it torn, opened, or a different weight than expected? Most shops allow you to refuse a damaged parcel on the spot. Take a photo if anything looks wrong.
  • Count and check the item. Open the parcel and check the item while the rider is still there if the seller allows it. If the item is wrong, contact the shop immediately — do not wait days.
  • Never pay in advance to a rider. COD means you pay when the parcel is in your hands. Anyone asking for advance payment by bank transfer or mobile wallet for a COD order is running a scam.
  • Keep the receipt. The delivery slip is your proof if you need a return or a refund.

Delivery timings also follow daily life. Riders usually work around prayer times and lunch, and deliveries slow down on Fridays around Jummah and during Ramadan evenings. If you want to plan your day around the delivery, check the prayer times for your city so you know when the rider is likely to pause.

Parcel scams to avoid

Parcel tracking is also used by scammers, because everyone is waiting for a parcel and everyone clicks tracking links. Watch for these:

  • Fake "your parcel is held" SMS. A message says your parcel is stuck and asks you to click a link and pay a small fee. Real couriers do not ask for payments through SMS links. Delete the message and check your tracking only on the courier's official website.
  • Fake courier calls. Someone calls, claims to be from a courier, and asks for an OTP, a bank code or an advance payment. Couriers never ask for OTPs. Hang up and call the courier's official helpline yourself.
  • Fake tracking websites. Search results can show lookalike sites with similar names. Type the courier's website address yourself, or use the link from your SMS or shopping app — never from a comment or forwarded message.
  • "Pay to release customs" tricks. Customs duty for a real parcel is paid through the courier's official channel, with a receipt. A random bank account or mobile wallet number is a scam.
  • Too-cheap online sellers. A shop offering branded goods at a fraction of the price and asking for advance payment will usually never send anything. Cash on delivery protects you.

If you receive a suspicious parcel message, learn how to check it before you click — the free guides on CyberSec Hub explain phishing and fake websites in plain language. And if a message already tricked you into sharing a code, change your passwords and contact your bank immediately.

Frequently asked questions

How long does a parcel take inside Pakistan?

Between major cities, most couriers deliver overnight or within one to two working days. Smaller towns take an extra day or two. Fuel prices and road conditions can stretch this, especially in the northern areas in winter — so always check the tracking rather than guessing a date.

Why has my tracking not updated for two days?

The parcel is usually in transit between scans. Trucks are not scanned on the road, so a Karachi-to-Lahore parcel can show no update for a day and then jump straight to "arrived". Worry only after three working days with no change.

Can I track a parcel without a tracking number?

Usually not. Some couriers can search by the sender's or receiver's phone number at their branch, but the tracking number is the only reliable way. Always ask the sender for it.

What if the status says "delivered" but I got nothing?

Check with family members and neighbours first — someone may have signed for it. Then call the courier with your tracking number and ask who signed and at what time. If no one in your house signed, file a complaint the same day.

Can I change the delivery address after sending?

Sometimes, if the parcel has not left the booking city yet. Call the courier's helpline quickly with the tracking number. Once the parcel is in transit, an address change is much harder, and the parcel may have to return to the sender first.

The bottom line

Live parcel tracking is simple once you know the three steps: keep your tracking number safe, check it only on the courier's official website, and read the scan timeline instead of guessing. A parcel that has not updated for a day is usually just on the road — a parcel that has not moved for three working days is worth a phone call. And on delivery day, check the parcel before you pay, and never share an OTP with anyone who claims to be a courier.
