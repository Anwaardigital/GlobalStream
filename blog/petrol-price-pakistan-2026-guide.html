Petrol Price in Pakistan: How It Is Set and How to Check It (2026 Guide)

By Anwaar Digital · Last updated: 19 November 2026 · Money & daily life

Few numbers affect daily life in Pakistan as directly as the petrol price. When it goes up, bus fares rise, food gets more expensive, and every family feels it — because almost everything we buy travels by road. When it comes down, there is a small sigh of relief across the whole country. Yet most people only ever see the final number on the pump board. They never see how that number is made.

This guide explains the petrol price in Pakistan in plain English: who sets it, how often it changes, what is inside every litre you buy, what makes it go up or down, and how to check the live price for free on GlobalStream's petrol price page. No jargon, no guesswork — just what you need to know before your next fill-up.

What is the petrol price?

The petrol price is the price of one litre of motor spirit — the fuel most cars, motorcycles and rickshaws run on — at a filling station. It is quoted per litre, in Pakistani rupees. Two numbers matter, and people often mix them up:

  • Ex-depot price. This is the price at which oil marketing companies sell fuel to filling stations from their depots. It is the "wholesale" price, and it is the number the government announces every fortnight.
  • Retail (pump) price. This is the price you actually pay. It is the ex-depot price plus the filling station's margin and any local costs. In practice, the pump price and the announced price are very close, because dealer margins are fixed and the same across the country.

Petrol is not the only regulated fuel. High-speed diesel (HSD) — used by trucks, buses, tractors and generators — is priced the same way and usually announced at the same time. Kerosene oil and light diesel oil are also regulated, though fewer people buy them. When people say "the petrol price went up", they usually mean the whole set of fuel prices moved together — and diesel matters just as much, because it sets the cost of transporting goods. How is the price set?

Petrol prices in Pakistan are revised every fortnight — on the 1st and the 16th of each month. The process works like this:

  • OGRA works out the numbers. The Oil and Gas Regulatory Authority (OGRA) calculates what the price should be, based on the cost of imported fuel, the exchange rate, and the fixed margins and taxes. OGRA then sends its working to the government.
  • The government decides. The Finance Division reviews OGRA's summary and announces the new prices. Sometimes the government absorbs part of an increase by cutting the petroleum levy, so the pump price rises less than the true cost — and sometimes it passes the full change on.
  • The new price takes effect at midnight. Filling stations switch their boards to the new rate, and the new price applies from that moment. That is why you sometimes see long queues at pumps on the evening of the 15th or the 30th — people filling up before a possible increase.

This fortnightly system means the price you pay can change up to 24 times a year. It also means the price is never a surprise for long: if world oil prices jump sharply in the first week of a fortnight, the effect usually shows up in the next announcement.

What is inside the price of one litre?

The price of a litre of petrol is not one number — it is a stack of smaller costs. Understanding the stack explains why the pump price is always much higher than the crude oil price you hear about on the news:

  • The fuel itself (import parity price). Pakistan imports most of its petrol and crude oil, priced in US dollars against international benchmarks such as Brent crude. This is the biggest single part of the price, and it moves with the world market.
  • Freight and handling. Getting fuel from a ship at the port to a depot, and then to a filling station, costs money — shipping, storage, and road transport. A margin called the Inland Freight Equalisation Margin (IFEM) is built in so that the pump price stays broadly the same across the country, whether the station is in Karachi or in a far northern town.
  • Oil company and dealer margins. The oil marketing company (OMC) and the filling station each earn a fixed, regulated margin per litre. These are small compared with the fuel cost, but they are what keep the pumps running.
  • Petroleum levy. The government charges a levy on every litre. This is a major source of revenue, and it is also the tool the government uses to soften price shocks: when world prices spike, the levy can be cut so the pump price does not jump as much.
  • Sales tax and other duties. Taxes are applied on top of the base price. The exact rates are set in the budget and can change, which is another reason the pump price does not move in lockstep with crude oil alone.

Because so much of the stack is fixed in rupees while the fuel itself is priced in dollars, the dollar-to-rupee exchange rate matters enormously. If the rupee weakens against the dollar, every litre costs more in rupees even if the world oil price has not moved at all. That is why petrol buyers watch the live currency rates just as closely as the oil market — and why the petrol price and the gold rate often move in the same direction, since both are priced in dollars internationally.

What makes the petrol price go up or down?

Six forces push the price in one direction or the other:

1. World crude oil prices

This is the biggest driver. When Brent crude rises — because of a supply cut, a conflict in an oil-producing region, or strong demand — Pakistan's import bill rises and the pump price usually follows at the next fortnightly revision. When crude falls, the relief usually follows too, though sometimes more slowly.

2. The dollar rate

Oil is bought in dollars and sold in rupees. A weaker rupee means more rupees for the same barrel of oil. Even a small move in the USD to PKR rate can add several rupees to a litre. This is the factor most people miss — and it is the reason the petrol price can rise even when world oil prices are flat.

3. Taxes and the petroleum levy

When the government raises the levy or a tax, the pump price rises without any change in the world market. When it cuts the levy to protect consumers, the pump price can stay flat even while crude is rising. Budget announcements are therefore worth watching if you drive every day.

4. World events and supply

Wars, sanctions, pipeline problems and decisions by big oil producers all move the world price. World news and Pakistan news often explain a price move before the official announcement does — if crude jumps after a supply disruption, a higher pump price at the next revision is likely.

5. Demand at home

When the economy grows and more vehicles are on the road, Pakistan imports more fuel. Demand does not set the price directly — the price is set by import cost and taxes — but high demand raises the import bill and puts pressure on the rupee, which feeds back into the price.

6. Refining and supply chain

Pakistan refines part of its fuel at home and imports the rest as finished petrol and diesel. Refinery shutdowns, port delays or transport problems can create short local shortages, and shortages push prices and queues up even when the official rate has not changed.

Petrol vs diesel: what is the difference?

Many families run both a petrol car and a diesel vehicle, or they pay fares set by diesel prices. The two fuels are priced differently for a reason:

  • Petrol (motor spirit) powers most private cars, motorcycles and rickshaws. It is lighter, burns faster, and is usually the fuel people check first.
  • High-speed diesel (HSD) powers trucks, buses, tractors and most generators. Diesel engines are more fuel-efficient, which is why heavy transport uses them. Diesel is usually priced a little differently from petrol because its import cost and taxes differ.
  • Why diesel matters to everyone. Even if you never buy diesel yourself, you pay for it: nearly every item in a shop arrived by diesel truck. A diesel increase raises transport costs, and transport costs raise food and goods prices. That is why a petrol price announcement is really a cost-of-living announcement.

How to save fuel — and money — every month

You cannot control the pump price, but you can control how much fuel you use. These habits genuinely cut consumption:

  • Drive smoothly. Gentle acceleration and steady speed use far less fuel than hard starts and sudden braking. On a motorway, a steady moderate speed beats speeding up and slowing down.
  • Keep tyres at the right pressure. Under-inflated tyres drag on the road and burn extra fuel. Check pressures at least once a month — the correct pressure is printed on a sticker inside the driver's door or in the owner's manual.
  • Do not idle for long. If you are waiting more than a minute or two, switching the engine off saves fuel. Long idling in traffic jams is one of the biggest hidden wastes.
  • Keep the engine tuned. A dirty air filter, old spark plugs or overdue engine oil all make the engine work harder. Regular servicing pays for itself in fuel saved.
  • Lighten the load. A roof rack, a boot full of heavy items, or open windows at high speed all increase drag and fuel use. Carry only what you need.
  • Plan your trips. Combining several errands into one trip uses less fuel than many short trips, because a cold engine burns more fuel in the first few minutes. Use weather and traffic information to avoid the worst congestion.
  • Consider alternatives. For short trips, walking or cycling costs nothing; for regular commutes, carpooling or public transport can cut your monthly fuel bill sharply. Petrol pump scams to watch out for

Most filling stations are honest, but a few tricks are common enough to know about:

  • The meter not starting at zero. Always check that the meter reads zero before fuelling starts. If it does not, ask the attendant to reset it in front of you.
  • Short measure. A pump that delivers less than it shows is cheating you. If your tank regularly takes less than expected, or your mileage suddenly drops after filling at one station, try a different pump and compare.
  • Adulterated fuel. Mixing cheaper solvents into petrol damages engines and wastes your money. Warning signs are rough running, poor mileage and a strange smell. Stick to well-known, busy stations — a station with long queues has more to lose from cheating.
  • Distraction tricks. While one attendant talks to you, another may stop the meter early or skip the reset. Watch the meter yourself from start to finish.
  • Fake discounts and advance-payment offers. Be cautious of anyone selling "cheap petrol" online or through social media, especially if they ask for advance payment. Frequently asked questions

How often does the petrol price change in Pakistan?

Every fortnight — on the 1st and the 16th of each month. The government announces the new rates based on OGRA's working, and they take effect from midnight.

Why does the petrol price rise when crude oil falls?

Because the pump price depends on more than crude oil. If the rupee has weakened against the dollar, or the government has raised the petroleum levy or a tax, the pump price can rise even while crude is falling. Check the live dollar rate — it usually explains the gap.

Why is the petrol price almost the same in every city?

Because of the Inland Freight Equalisation Margin (IFEM). It spreads transport costs evenly, so a litre costs broadly the same in Karachi and in a small northern town. Without it, fuel would be much cheaper near the ports and much more expensive far away.

Why does a petrol increase make food more expensive?

Because food travels by diesel truck. When diesel rises, transport costs rise, and shopkeepers pass those costs on. A fuel increase is therefore felt in the price of almost everything, not just at the pump.

The bottom line

The petrol price in Pakistan is set every fortnight from the world oil price, the dollar rate, fixed margins and government taxes — and OGRA's working is what ties them together. You cannot change the price, but you can understand it: check the live rate with its last-updated time, watch the dollar rate alongside it, and know what is inside every litre.
